## 1. BUSINESS OVERVIEW

PepsiCo, Inc. (NASDAQ: PEP) was formed through the 1965 merger of Pepsi-Cola and Frito-Lay, and expanded into foods via the 1998 acquisition of Tropicana and the 2001 merger with Quaker Oats (bringing Gatorade). Headquarters: Purchase, NY.

**Core value proposition**: The only major CPG company with a #1 position in both salty snacks and carbonated soft drinks in the U.S. — the "Power of One" model bundles shelf space, promotional real estate, and DSD route density to give retailers an unmatched category-management partner.

**Key brands by segment**:
- *Frito-Lay North America (FLNA)*: Lay's, Doritos, Cheetos, Tostitos, Ruffles, Fritos, SunChips, PopCorners
- *Quaker Foods North America (QFNA)*: Quaker Oats, Cap'n Crunch, Life, Rice-A-Roni
- *PepsiCo Beverages North America (PBNA)*: Pepsi, Mountain Dew, Gatorade, Lipton (JV), Aquafina, Bubly, Rockstar, Tropicana (partial stake retained), Poppi (acquired 2026 — see §5)
- *International (PFSA/AMESA/APAC)*: All above brands plus local brands

**Revenue/profit mix (FY2025)**:
- Total revenue: $93.9 billion ([Bakery & Snacks, 2026-03-24](https://www.bakeryandsnacks.com/Article/2026/03/24/pepsico-cuts-skus-and-bets-on-protein-snacks-in-major-2026-strategy-reset/))
- Operating profit: $11.5 billion (declining in FY2025 vs. FY2024)
- FLNA is structurally the highest-margin segment (estimated ~27–29% operating margin) [Estimate]; beverages (PBNA) carry lower margins (~10–12%) [Estimate]
- Snacks + foods represent roughly 55–57% of total revenue; beverages roughly 43–45% [Estimate based on segment filings]

**DSD Network**: PepsiCo operates one of the largest DSD (direct-store-delivery) networks in North America — 300,000+ retail routes. Out-of-stock rates for DSD products are 2–4% lower than warehouse-distributed peers. ([Food Dive](https://www.fooddive.com/news/as-cpg-distribution-shifts-pepsico-reaffirms-its-preference-for-dsd/436989/))

## 2. MANAGEMENT & LEADERSHIP

### CEO: Ramon Laguarta
- Born 1963, Barcelona. MBA from ESADE Business School; Master of Management from Thunderbird School of Global Management.
- Joined PepsiCo January 1996; prior roles: CEO of Europe & Sub-Saharan Africa, PepsiCo President.
- Became CEO October 3, 2018 — the sixth CEO in company history and first Spanish CEO of a major U.S. multinational.
- As of 2026, also serves as Chairman of the Board.
- Source: [Ramón Laguarta — Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramon_Laguarta); [ESADE Award 2026](https://www.esade.edu/en/news/ramon-laguarta-chairman-the-board-and-ceo-pepsico-winner-2026-esade-award)

### CFO Transition
- **Jamie Caulfield** (30+ year PepsiCo veteran) retired from CFO role effective November 10, 2025; served as adviser through May 15, 2026.
- **Steve Schmitt** appointed EVP & CFO effective November 10, 2025. Background: EVP & CFO of Walmart U.S. (2016–2025), prior roles at Yum! Brands and UPS. Hired specifically for supply-chain, omnichannel, and cost-discipline expertise.
- Sources: [StockTitan announcement](https://www.stocktitan.net/news/PEP/pepsi-co-announces-new-chief-financial-nx1y7kwb3w91.html); [BusinessChief profile](https://businesschief.com/news/what-pepsico-sees-in-walmarts-steve-schmitt-as-new-cfo)

### Capital Allocation
- **Dividend King**: 54 consecutive annual dividend increases as of 2026. Annualized dividend raised 4% to $5.92/share (effective June 2026 payment). CAGR of quarterly dividend ~7.3% over the past decade.
- Source: [InvestSnips PEP dividend analysis](https://investsnips.com/pep-dividend/)
- Buyback program: active but subordinated to dividend commitment and M&A investment in 2025–2026 period.
- Debt: elevated following Siete Foods ($1.2B, closed Jan 2025) and Poppi ($1.95B, announced/closing 2026) acquisitions.

### Elliott Activist Engagement
- Elliott Investment Management disclosed a ~$4 billion stake and engaged constructively with management.
- Elliott's Marc Steinberg publicly welcomed PepsiCo's shareholder-value plan: *"We believe this plan will drive greater revenue and profit growth"* and supported supply-chain review and board refreshment commitment.
- PepsiCo announced the shareholder-value priorities in conjunction with constructive engagement — no hostile proxy context.
- Sources: [PepsiCo IR press release](https://www.pepsico.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2025/pepsico-announces-priorities-to-enhance-shareholder-value-and-provides-preliminary-2026-outlook); [SEC 8-K FY2026](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000077476/000007747626000009/q420258-kxexhibit991.htm)

### Guidance Track Record
- FY2025 was a miss year for North American Foods (organic revenue declined in FLNA and QFNA).
- FY2026 guidance reaffirmed at Q1 2026 call: 2–4% organic growth, 5–7% core EPS growth.
- Q1 2026 delivered 2.6% organic revenue growth and core EPS +9% — in-line to slight beat on EPS.

## 4. COMPETITIVE MOAT & LANDSCAPE

### Morningstar Moat Rating: **Wide**
> "PepsiCo's tight retail relationships on the back of strong beverage and snack brands, coupled with massive distribution and procurement scale, underpin our wide moat rating."
- Source: [Morningstar PEP analysis](https://www.morningstar.com/stocks/this-dividend-aristocrat-is-buyheres-why)

### Moat Sources
1. **Brand equity**: Lay's, Doritos, Gatorade, Pepsi are global icons with decades of consumer loyalty and marketing investment.
2. **DSD scale**: 300,000+ retail routes; 2–4% lower out-of-stock vs. warehouse peers; real-time AI-optimized inventory across routes (generative AI integration, early 2025).
3. **Power of One / shelf dominance**: Combined snack + beverage selling gives PepsiCo unmatched negotiating leverage with retailers for shelf space and promotional programming.
4. **Scale cost advantage**: $93.9B revenue base enables procurement leverage, plant utilization, and R&D investment that smaller peers cannot match.
5. **Category leadership**: Frito-Lay holds ~60% share of the U.S. salty snack market. [Estimate; cited in [Food Business News](https://www.foodbusinessnews.net/articles/26375-slowing-salty-snack-sales-hit-pepsicos-frito-lay)]

### Moat Vulnerabilities
- Private label trade-down (accelerated by consumer value-seeking post-inflation)
- GLP-1 demand destruction for calorie-dense snacks (see §6)
- Health/regulatory pressure on artificial dyes, HFCS, sodium (see §6)
- Beverage moat weaker than snacks — KO holds higher CSD market share globally

### Competitive Landscape

| Competitor | Segment Overlap | Approx. Share vs. PEP | Moat / Threat Read |
|---|---|---|---|
| Coca-Cola (KO) | Beverages (CSD) | KO ~20% U.S. CSD; PEP ~16% | Asset-light bottler model yields superior beverage margins; strongest CSD brand globally; primary threat in beverages |
| Mondelez (MDLZ) | Biscuits, chocolate, savory snacks | Modest in U.S. salty snacks | Strong in global biscuits (Oreo, Ritz); pushing into well-being snacks — competes on premium shelf |
| General Mills (GIS) | Snacks, cereals, frozen | Modest overlap | Broad CPG peer; competes on brand + innovation; Nature Valley, Fiber One in health snacks |
| Keurig Dr Pepper (KDP) | Beverages (CSDs, flavored) | ~7% U.S. CSD | U.S. CSD #3; strength in flavor-led and at-home brewing; pressures PEP in flavor innovation |
| Kellanova (K) | Salty snacks, crackers | Modest | Pringles is a direct Frito-Lay competitor; Mars acquisition (2024) adds scale |
| Campbell's | Crackers, Goldfish | Minimal in salty snacks | Pepperidge Farm / Goldfish competes in snack crackers; limited direct threat |
| Private Label | All categories | Growing | Accelerating in cookies, crackers, chips; biggest structural threat to volume share in value channels |

## 5. STRATEGIC GROWTH INITIATIVES

### 5a. Productivity & Margin Expansion (2026–2028)
- Record productivity savings targeted FY2026 via automation, digitalization, simplification.
- 20% SKU reduction in U.S. (~5,000 SKUs removed); 3 manufacturing plant closures completed.
- 100+ bps core operating margin expansion targeted over 2026–2028.
- Sources: [PepsiCo IR](https://www.pepsico.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2025/pepsico-announces-priorities-to-enhance-shareholder-value-and-provides-preliminary-2026-outlook); [Bakery & Snacks strategy reset](https://www.bakeryandsnacks.com/Article/2026/03/24/pepsico-cuts-skus-and-bets-on-protein-snacks-in-major-2026-strategy-reset/)

### 5b. Portfolio Transformation — Functional / Better-For-You
- **Doritos Protein**: 10g protein per serving; first protein-forward launch in the Frito-Lay portfolio.
- **Pepsi Prebiotic Cola**, **Smartfood FiberPop**, **SunChips Fiber**: target fiber + digestive health trends.
- **Simply NKD Cheetos and Doritos**: no artificial colors or flavors; direct response to MAHA/RFK Jr. scrutiny.
- Laguarta: expects fiber to be "the next protein" in consumer preferences.
- Source: [Bakery & Snacks 2026 reset](https://www.bakeryandsnacks.com/Article/2026/03/24/pepsico-cuts-skus-and-bets-on-protein-snacks-in-major-2026-strategy-reset/)

### 5c. Acquisitions — Portfolio Entry into Functional Beverages and Hispanic Foods
- **Siete Foods** ($1.2B acquisition, closed January 2025): grain-free, Mexican-American heritage snack and food brand. Expands FLNA into cleaner-label, high-growth multicultural segment.
- **Poppi** ($1.95B acquisition, announced 2026): prebiotic soda brand (apple cider vinegar + prebiotics, 5g sugar/can). Positions PEP in the rapidly growing functional/gut-health soda category alongside competitor Olipop.
- Sources: [Marketing Dive on Poppi](https://www.marketingdive.com/news/pepsico-buys-poppi-2B-pepsi-purchase-prebiotic-healthy-soda-better-for-you-drink-olipop-simply-pop/742679/); [Nutraceuticals World](https://www.nutraceuticalsworld.com/breaking-news/pepsico-deals-1-95-billion-to-acquire-prebiotic-soda-brand-poppi/)

### 5d. Away-From-Home / Foodservice Acceleration
- FLNA driving unit volume through foodservice channel expansion; "away-from-home acceleration" cited by Laguarta as a key Q1 2026 volume driver.
- Source: [Q1 2026 transcript](https://www.fool.com/earnings/call-transcripts/2026/04/16/pepsico-pep-q1-2026-earnings-call-transcript/)

### 5e. International Expansion
- International segment cited by Laguarta as "very solid, continues to accelerate." No demand softness observed internationally vs. NA headwinds.
- LATAM, AMESA, and APAC remain growth vectors; Frito-Lay international brand rollout ongoing.
- Source: [Q1 2026 transcript](https://www.fool.com/earnings/call-transcripts/2026/04/16/pepsico-pep-q1-2026-earnings-call-transcript/)

### 5f. AI + Digital in DSD
- Generative AI integrated across DSD network in early 2025, optimizing real-time inventory for 300,000+ retail routes.
- Source: [Yahoo Finance on DSD network](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/pepsico-three-channel-distribution-network-170022730.html)

### 5g. pep+ Sustainability / ESG
- "pep+" is PepsiCo's end-to-end strategic framework: sustainable agriculture, packaging, value chain emissions. Cited in annual reports as a brand and retailer-relationship driver.

## 6. KEY RISKS (Materiality-Filtered per Rule #25)

### R1 — GLP-1 Demand Destruction for Calorie-Dense Snacks
- **Impact**: High if adoption scales. Barclays and Morgan Stanley estimate GLP-1 users could reduce total U.S. calorie consumption by 3–5% by 2030. Chips spending among GLP-1 users down ~11%; sweet bakery down ~7%.
- **Probability**: Moderate near-term; high 5-year tail risk.
- **Priced in?**: Partially — market has repriced CPG/snacks broadly, but secular trajectory uncertain.
- **Differentiation**: More severe for Frito-Lay than any other CPG segment given ~60% U.S. salty snack dominance.
- Sources: [Indigrowth GLP-1 snack impact](https://www.indigrowth.com/weight-loss-pills-are-eating-snack-sales/); [Bakery & Snacks GLP-1 reshape](https://www.bakeryandsnacks.com/Article/2025/08/28/glp-1-weightloss-drugs-reshape-global-bakery-snack-sales/)

### R2 — Frito-Lay North America Volume Weakness / Price Elasticity
- FLNA reported organic revenue decline in FY2025; consumer pushback on post-inflation price increases.
- Price elasticity greater than historically assumed — PEP had to cut prices in early 2026 to restore volume.
- Sources: [Marketplace on price cuts](https://www.marketplace.org/story/2026/02/03/pepsico-slashes-prices-on-snacks-in-bid-to-boost-sales/); [Food Business News FLNA slowdown](https://www.foodbusinessnews.net/articles/26375-slowing-salty-snack-sales-hit-pepsicos-frito-lay)
- **Priced in?**: Partially — reflected in 2025 earnings misses and stock underperformance. Q1 2026 volume recovery (+2%) is early positive signal.

### R3 — MAHA / Regulatory Scrutiny of Artificial Dyes and Additives
- FDA/HHS under RFK Jr. targeting phase-out of all petroleum-based synthetic food dyes by end of 2026.
- PEP already reformulating: Simply NKD Doritos, Cheetos, Lay's, and Tostitos to remove artificial flavors/colors.
- Reformulation carries incremental COGS; brand disruption risk if reformulated products lose consumer affinity.
- **Priced in?**: No — timeline and scope of FDA enforcement remain uncertain; reformulation costs not fully disclosed.
- Sources: [Quench Agency on dyes](https://quenchagency.com/rfk-jr-and-the-end-of-artificial-dyes-is-reshaping-the-aisle/); [NewTarget on PEP reformulation](https://www.newstarget.com/2025-07-22-pepsico-ditches-artifical-dyes-flavorings-lays-tostitos.html)

### R4 — Private Label Trade-Down
- Post-inflation consumer value-seeking accelerating private label share in crackers, chips, and cookies.
- Most acute in value channels (Aldi, Lidl, dollar stores) where Frito-Lay distribution advantage is thinner.
- **Priced in?**: Partially — structural rather than cyclical; not fully embedded in consensus models.

### R5 — Input Cost / Tariff Volatility
- Corn, potatoes, vegetable oils, aluminum (cans), and PET resin are key inputs; tariff exposure on imported packaging and agricultural inputs.
- FY2025 COGS pressure contributed to margin compression; 2026 productivity savings intended to offset.
- **Priced in?**: Moderately — manageable given hedging program but escalation risk with trade policy uncertainty.

### R6 — FX Headwinds
- International segment (PFSA, AMESA, APAC) contributes significant revenue; strong USD reduces reported revenue and EPS.
- FY2025 FX was a notable earnings drag.
- **Priced in?**: Yes — standard CPG FX risk; well-understood by market.

### R7 — Activist-Driven Strategic Risk (Elliott)
- Elliott's $4B stake creates ongoing pressure to deliver on the 2026–2028 plan; underperformance could escalate to board-level intervention.
- Board refreshment commitment made; if organic growth target (2–4%) is missed again, strategic alternatives (spin-off of beverages vs. snacks) could be forced.
- **Not priced in**: Market currently treats this as constructive; forced structural separation would be a regime change.
- Source: [PepsiCo IR shareholder priorities](https://www.pepsico.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2025/pepsico-announces-priorities-to-enhance-shareholder-value-and-provides-preliminary-2026-outlook)

### R8 — Quaker Recall / Reputational Hangover
- Quaker Foods segment has faced regulatory and quality issues (2024 recall); segment remains structurally subscale and a drag on portfolio growth.
- **Priced in?**: Yes — market discounts Quaker.
