## Where LOW Wins

**1. Asymmetry.** PW EV +50% vs. spot ($322/$214) versus HD's +18% ($377/$320). The probability-weighted expected return gap is significant: LOW is priced for the bear; HD is priced for a moderate base case.

**2. Valuation multiple.** ≈17× FY2026E P/E vs. HD's ≈21×. With both companies facing the same macro headwind (housing lock-in) and similar comp trajectories, paying 4× fewer turns of earnings for LOW implies either the market is underpricing LOW's recovery leverage or overpricing HD's distribution premium. At current prices, the answer is likely both.

**3. Balance sheet.** LOW carries ~$16B net debt (manageable; FBM acquisition at 13.4× EBITDA is the chief concern). HD carries ~$42B. The leverage gap means LOW restarts buybacks now; HD is suspended for potentially 2+ more years. Every dollar of FCF at LOW is worth more to per-share value right now.

**4. Execution simplicity.** LOW's thesis is a classic retail turnaround + housing recovery. HD's thesis requires three things simultaneously: housing recovery, SRS integration success, and GMS/Mingledorff's margin assimilation. More things to get right = more surface area for disappointment.

**5. R/R.** ≈4.9:1 (LOW) vs. ≈2.0:1 (HD). At the same macro thesis (housing unlock), LOW pays nearly 2.5× more per unit of upside risk.

---

## Where HD Wins

**1. Moat depth.** HD's scale advantage is real and widening. ≈33% US market share vs. LOW's ≈17%; 2,359 stores + 1,250+ SRS branches + GMS + Mingledorff's = a four-node Pro ecosystem with no peer. Lowe's is narrowing comps directionally but the absolute gap remains enormous.

**2. Pro distribution infrastructure.** SRS + GMS + Mingledorff's gives HD a $50B+ addressable TAM extension into specialty trade distribution (roofing, pool, landscaping, gypsum, HVAC). Lowe's has no equivalent. If this thesis delivers $500M–$1B in EBITDA synergies by FY2028, HD is the more powerful compounder.

**3. Revenue scale + operating leverage.** When housing recovers, HD's operating leverage math is more dramatic: a 200bps margin recovery on $185B revenue = ≈$3.5B additional operating income. The leverage cuts both ways, but the upside is also bigger.

**4. Q1 binary behind it.** The pre-print uncertainty risk is gone. Q1 FY2026 delivered a slight beat, guidance reaffirmed, and the stock is 7.5% off the floor. For investors who wanted to wait for the Q1 clarity — that clarity has arrived.

**5. Analyst consensus convergence.** Post-Q1 median target ≈$370 is closely aligned with PW EV $377. This is unusual — it means the market's consensus and the valuation model are telling the same story, reducing the risk of a "consensus surprise" working against you.

## The Key Trade-Off

**LOW is the better trade if** you believe:
- Housing recovery is a matter of *when*, not *if*, and LOW's simpler execution makes it a cleaner beneficiary
- The multiple gap (17× vs. 21×) is too wide for two companies with the same core macro driver
- Balance sheet conservatism matters more than distribution optionality over the next 3 years

**HD is the better trade if** you believe:
- SRS integration delivers promised synergies — in which case HD is building a category-defining business that LOW cannot replicate
- Pro market share shifts toward the four-node ecosystem and away from retail-only models permanently
- The quality premium (wider moat, more durable earnings power) justifies paying 4× turns above LOW

**The resolution**: Both are appropriate. LOW wins on pure near-term asymmetry; HD wins on the long-duration compounding bet. In a recovery scenario, **LOW likely delivers higher percentage returns from current prices**; in a stress scenario, **HD's moat provides a more durable earnings floor**. Neither is cheap in absolute terms — both are reasonable in the context of the housing-recovery thesis.
