## Business Overview

PepsiCo, Inc. (NASDAQ: PEP) is one of the world's largest food and beverage companies, assembled through three defining transactions: the 1965 Pepsi-Cola/Frito-Lay merger, the 1998 Tropicana acquisition, and the 2001 Quaker Oats/Gatorade merger. **PEP is the only major consumer-packaged-goods company holding simultaneous #1 positions in both U.S. salty snacks (Frito-Lay) and a top-two position in U.S. beverages.** FY2025 net revenue was $93.9B. Its brand portfolio spans Lay's, Doritos, Cheetos, Tostitos, Ruffles (Frito-Lay); Quaker, Gatorade, Tropicana stakes; and Pepsi, Mountain Dew, Mtn Dew, Aquafina, Bubly, Rockstar, and Starbucks ready-to-drink (beverages). Headquartered in Purchase, NY.

**Business model**: Brand-premium pricing on everyday snacks and beverages, distributed largely through PepsiCo's own direct-store-delivery (DSD) network of 300,000+ routes. The "Power of One" advantage bundles snacks + beverages into a single shelf-space and promotional package no single-category peer can match. Snacks/foods are ≈55–57% of revenue but carry far higher margins (FLNA ≈22–26% operating margin) than beverages (PBNA ≈4–9%), so the snacks business drives the majority of profit. Revenue is subscription-like in character — consumers re-purchase continuously — giving extreme stability but limiting high-growth potential.

**Current leadership**: Ramon Laguarta has been CEO since October 2018 (also Chairman). Steve Schmitt became CFO effective November 10, 2025, recruited from Walmart U.S. (where he was EVP/CFO). In 2025, Elliott Investment Management disclosed a ≈$4B stake and engaged constructively; PepsiCo responded with a shareholder-value plan (productivity acceleration, board refreshment, portfolio focus) that Elliott publicly endorsed.

## Pivotal Investment Question

> **Is PepsiCo's 2024–2026 de-rating (from ≈$196 in 2023 to ≈$127–$150) a cyclical overreaction to GLP-1 fear + temporary Frito-Lay volume softness + activist noise, or does it reflect genuine structural impairment of snacking demand and pricing power that justifies a permanently lower (≈14–15×) multiple?**

Q1 FY2026 offered the first real counter-evidence: Frito-Lay North America returned to +2% volume growth (Lay's specifically), international segments accelerated sharply (EMEA +18%, LatAm Foods +16% reported), and management reaffirmed the FY2026 organic +2–4% / mid-single-digit core EPS growth guide while CFO Schmitt detailed a productivity program targeting 100+ bps of operating-margin expansion through 2028. But the bear levers are real and not yet resolved: GLP-1 adoption is a multi-year demand question with PEP the most exposed food name; the FDA/MAHA synthetic-dye phase-out forces reformulation of core brands by end-2026; and the PBNA beverage segment's margin collapse to 3.9% (from 9.4% in FY2023) is the single largest drag on consolidated profitability. The question resolves over the next 2–4 earnings cycles: if FLNA volume holds positive, international stays strong, and the productivity walk lifts PBNA margins back toward 6–8%, the multiple re-rates toward 18–20×. If volume reverts and GLP-1 erosion accelerates, the structural thesis wins at 14–15×.

## Section 3 — Competitive Moat & Landscape

**Moat Rating: Wide** (Morningstar — [source](https://www.morningstar.com/stocks/this-dividend-aristocrat-is-buyheres-why))

### Moat Sources

- **Brand equity** (Strong): Lay's, Doritos, Cheetos, Gatorade, and Pepsi are category-defining global brands. Frito-Lay holds an estimated ≈60% of the U.S. salty-snack market (*[Estimate, Food Business News](https://www.foodbusinessnews.net/articles/26375-slowing-salty-snack-sales-hit-pepsicos-frito-lay)*) — a concentration no peer approaches.
- **Direct-store-delivery (DSD) scale** (Strong): 300,000+ DSD routes give PepsiCo 2–4% lower out-of-stock rates than warehouse-delivered peers and direct control of shelf placement and in-store execution. DSD is expensive to operate but extremely hard to replicate (*[Food Dive](https://www.fooddive.com/news/as-cpg-distribution-shifts-pepsico-reaffirms-its-preference-for-dsd/436989/)*).
- **"Power of One" scale** (Strong): Bundling snacks + beverages into a single shelf and promotional package for retailers is a structural advantage versus single-category competitors (KO in beverages, MDLZ/GIS in snacks).
- **Procurement and manufacturing scale** (Moderate-Strong): A $93.9B revenue base yields purchasing leverage on corn, potatoes, oils, aluminum, and PET resin.
- **Pricing power** (Strong historically; stressed currently): Mid-single-digit price increases funded the FY2022–FY2023 organic surge but came at the cost of volume; FY2025 required price reductions on some snacks to restore volume — the FY2026 challenge is volume-led recovery without ceding the price base.

**Moat vulnerabilities**:
- GLP-1 weight-loss drugs threaten the highest-margin, highest-share category (salty snacks) more directly than any peer.
- Private label gains share fastest in value/discount channels (Aldi, Lidl, dollar stores) where the DSD advantage thins.
- Persistent premium pricing during inflation accelerated private-label trial; quality parity is narrowing in some snack and beverage sub-categories.

## Section 5 — Strategic Growth Initiatives

## Section 7 — Industry-Specific Macro Analysis

**Consumer staples sector dynamics**: PEP is a defensive staple (beta 0.39) that typically outperforms in recessions and lags bull markets. FY2024–FY2026 is atypical: PEP has underperformed even within staples because the headwinds are partly intrinsic (volume softness, beverage-margin erosion, GLP-1 exposure) rather than purely macro.

**GLP-1 adoption (highest-impact structural macro factor)**: The dominant multi-year variable. Rising GLP-1 penetration reduces caloric intake, with early data showing snack and CSD consumption falling among users. A 1–2 standard-deviation faster adoption curve could compress U.S. snacking volume by mid-single digits by 2030 — disproportionately hitting FLNA, PEP's profit engine. The bull counter is that PEP's functional-snack innovation (protein, fiber) and away-from-home channel capture offset the at-home decline.

**Health / regulatory (MAHA)**: The FDA/HHS synthetic-dye phase-out by end-2026 is a near-term operational macro factor — reformulation costs and consumer-acceptance risk across core Frito-Lay brands. Added-sugar scrutiny and potential soda taxes are slower-moving regulatory tails for PBNA.

**Beverage competitive intensity**: KO and KDP are pressuring PBNA on innovation and channel; the segment's 3.9% margin reflects both internal cost issues and external competition. Recovery depends as much on category positioning as on productivity.

**FX and EM consumer**: ≈44% international means EUR/BRL/MXN/GBP moves materially affect reported results; a strong dollar is a recurring headwind. EM consumer strength (LatAm, AMESA, APAC) is currently a tailwind offsetting U.S. softness.

**Interest-rate sensitivity**: At ≈17× forward core P/E and a ≈4% yield, PEP is moderately rate-sensitive as a bond-proxy staple. Rate cuts would support the multiple and the dividend's relative attractiveness; sustained high rates cap re-rating.

Conversely, the genuine analytical uncertainty is the terminal GLP-1 impact on FLNA volume, which is hard to model and bounds the downside.
