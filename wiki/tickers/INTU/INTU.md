## Business Overview

Intuit (NASDAQ: INTU) is a vertical SaaS platform that serves consumers, small and mid-market businesses, and accountants across four product lines: **TurboTax** (consumer and assisted tax preparation, ≈70% of U.S. DIY e-filed returns), **QuickBooks** (de-facto SMB accounting standard; cloud engine is QuickBooks Online — QBO — with ≈7.5M paying subscribers internationally), **Credit Karma** (free credit scores and financial-product marketplace, 100M+ U.S. members, acquired 2020 for $8.1B), and **Mailchimp** (email/marketing automation, acquired 2021 for $12B, now integrated within the Global Business Solutions Group as the marketing layer). FY2025 revenue was **$18.83B** (+15.6% YoY). After the Q3 FY26 print, FY2026 full-year guidance was **raised to $21.341B–$21.374B** (+13–14% YoY), with non-GAAP EPS lifted to **$23.80–$23.85** (from $22.98–$23.18). (*[Intuit Q3 FY26 press release, May 21, 2026](https://investors.intuit.com/news-events/press-releases/detail/1312/intuit-reports-strong-third-quarter-results-and-raises-full-year-revenue-guidance)*)

The company has repositioned as an **"AI-driven expert platform"**: in FY2026 Intuit formalized a multiyear partnership with **Anthropic** and added all four core apps to **OpenAI's GPT directory**, while launching proprietary **Intuit Assist** agentic workflows across QBO and TurboTax. Management's framing — pairing AI with human intelligence (HI) for high-stakes financial decisions — is structurally different from the horizontal-LLM threat narrative that drove the stock down. CEO Goodarzi crystallized it on the Q3 call: *"Customers buy confidence not code. Which is why they spend at least 7x more on accounting and tax experts than on software alone."* Alongside the Q3 print, Intuit announced a **≈17% workforce reduction (≈3,000+ roles, ≈$300M restructuring charge)**, framing it as a refocusing on AI and the mid-market rather than a demand-driven cut. (*[Q3 FY26 earnings transcript, May 21, 2026](https://www.theglobeandmail.com/investing/markets/stocks/INTU/pressreleases/2049766/intuit-intu-q3-2026-earnings-transcript/)*; *[CNBC — 17% workforce cut](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/05/20/intuit-ceo-says-companys-17percent-workforce-cut-had-nothing-to-do-with-ai.html)*)

## Section 7 — Industry-Specific Macro Analysis

**U.S. Tax Preparation TAM**: The U.S. consumer and SMB tax preparation market is approximately $15B annually. Intuit captures ≈25–30% by returns and a disproportionately higher share of revenue (closer to 40–50% of industry revenue) because TurboTax commands premium ARPU in the DIY segment and grows its assisted tier. Total U.S. individual tax returns: ≈160M per year; approximately 80M are e-filed DIY; TurboTax serves ≈55–60M of those. The structural trend is toward DIY over professional preparers, which favors Intuit's model. **The government counter-trend reversed in late 2025**: IRS Direct File was permanently terminated and was unavailable for the 2026 season — removing the most-feared government free alternative. The residual competitive pressure is now purely commercial (Cash App Taxes, FreeTaxUSA undercutting the low-income DIY tier).

**SMB Software and Payroll TAM**: The U.S. SMB accounting, payroll, and payments market is a $50B+ opportunity. Intuit is the dominant player at small-business scale but sub-10% penetrated in the $10M–$500M mid-market that IES is targeting. The mid-market TAM expansion is the primary valuation re-rating catalyst.

**AI Macro**: The horizontal LLM narrative has compressed Intuit's multiple from ≈35× to ≈13.4× non-GAAP P/E — more than a 60% de-rating. The bull counter — validated by Q3 FY26 data (+10% revenue, raised guide) — is that vertical software with proprietary data, workflow integration, and a human-expertise layer is more valuable in an AI world, not less. The SMB finance use case specifically requires accuracy and liability accountability that general LLMs cannot currently provide ("what they are very focused on is in their high-stakes decisions...getting it wrong means huge liabilities" — Goodarzi, Q2 FY26). The risk is that this framing holds for 2–3 more years and then begins to erode as LLMs become auditable.

**Credit Cycle**: Credit Karma's revenue correlates closely with personal lending and credit card origination volumes. The FY2022–FY2024 tightening suppressed Credit Karma growth; the FY2025–FY2026 re-opening is the swing factor. A recession would materially compress Credit Karma (estimated 30–40% revenue decline in a severe scenario) but Intuit's overall business would remain profitable given GBSG and Consumer's recession-resilience (tax filings are legally mandatory).

**Regulatory Environment**: The FTC's prior deceptive advertising case against TurboTax was dismissed by an appeals court in early 2026. The IRS Direct File program — previously the primary regulatory headwind — was **permanently terminated by the administration in late 2025** and was not offered for the 2026 filing season; the One Big Beautiful Bill Act directs Treasury to study alternatives, and IRS Free File (AGI ≤ $89K) remains, but the threatening Direct File build-out is over. This is a policy decision and could in principle be revived under a future administration, but the near-to-medium-term overhang is removed. *[Source: Federal News Network, Nov 2025](https://federalnewsnetwork.com/it-modernization/2025/11/irs-direct-file-will-not-be-available-in-2026-agency-tells-states/)*
