## Business Overview

Lowe's is the #2 US home improvement retailer (1,759 large-format stores as of January 2026) serving DIY consumers and professional contractors through ~112,000 sq ft warehouse stores. Founded in 1921 in North Wilkesboro, NC; transformed into a national big-box chain in the mid-1990s. Since CEO Marvin Ellison joined in July 2018, the strategy has focused on three phases: (1) exit non-core operations — Canada divested Feb 2023; (2) build the Pro channel — Pro grew from ~19% of sales (FY2019) to ~30% (FY2025); (3) expand beyond retail into distribution and installation. The two FY2025 acquisitions — Artisan Design Group ($1.32B, flooring/design installation for homebuilders) and Foundation Building Materials ($8.8B, interior building materials distribution with 370+ North American branches) — represent a fundamental business model shift. Lowe's is no longer a pure retailer; it is building a "cradle-to-completion" new home construction platform.

## 3. Competitive Moat & Landscape

### Moat Rating: **Narrow**

Lowe's scale (~29–32% US home improvement retail market share at $84B revenue) confers purchasing leverage and distribution density but is materially narrower than Home Depot's (~47–51% share at $165B). HD's four-node Pro ecosystem (retail + SRS Distribution + HD Supply + GMS) has no equivalent at Lowe's today, though FBM + ADG are the first step toward building one. Lowe's moat rests on:

1. **Scale purchasing leverage**: ~$84B in annual merchandise purchasing provides meaningful supplier economics and private-label margin advantages that smaller peers cannot replicate
2. **Pro loyalty switching costs (moderate)**: MyLowe's Pro Rewards + trade credit + dedicated sales reps create re-order friction for repeat contractors
3. **Brand + Appliances category leadership**: ≈14% of sales in Appliances — a DIY-friendly category where HD does not have a dominant position; less contested and higher-ticket

**Where the moat faces pressure**: HD's revenue is ~1.95× Lowe's; HD's Pro mix is ~55% (retail) vs. LOW's ~30% retail; HD's SRS + GMS acquisitions ($24B combined) created a distribution infrastructure that Lowe's had zero equivalent to before FBM/ADG. The period through FY2028 is the evidence-gathering window for whether FBM closes the gap.

**Verdict**: Moat is real but narrow vs. HD. Lowe's Appliances + DIY category position defends well; FBM + ADG are the bet that a sufficient Pro distribution layer can be built via M&A. HD retains the wide moat; LOW's moat could widen materially if FBM integration succeeds — the current narrow rating reflects the uncertainty, not a permanent judgment.

Base case: gradual normalization begins FY2027; meaningful DIY project recovery is a FY2028+ story. — [NAHB 2026 Housing](https://www.nahb.org/news-and-economics/press-releases/2026/02/2026-housing-outlook-ongoing-challenges-cautious-optimism-and-incremental-gains) · [JP Morgan](https://www.jpmorgan.com/insights/global-research/real-estate/us-housing-market-outlook)
